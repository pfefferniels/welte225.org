A take

A few bars of the roll are played. Listen to them some times; then record, and while they play, hum the voice you hear leading and tap the beat on any key. The pitch does not matter: how the voice swells, where it turns, where you breathe. Wear headphones, so that the microphone hears only you.

loading the roll …



      

        The voice is read back into a body: VocalTractLab sings the take again
        and again, its lungs, vocal folds and lips corrected each time by how
        its sound differs from yours, until the copy lies on the take. What the
        body did then, where its effort grew and turned and where it breathed,
        says where one instruction ends and the next begins and what happens
        between the notes; the taps say where the beat falls where no note
        sounds, and on which note of a spread chord. The rest is fitted to the
        roll. The calls saved are MPM Desk's own, for its chain to write the MPM
        from. What the tempo and the dynamics leave is read too: the notes
        within a beat against the tempo as rubato, the chords against the
        dynamics as accents. All of it is written as MPM, which espressivo
        performs with its expression as it was read, taken away, or exaggerated.
