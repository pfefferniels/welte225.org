The humming

What a pianist might hum while playing: a bass line that moves between melody, tenor and bass, within about an octave, with a breath wherever it changes hands. It is written out in MEI, and each sung note takes its timing and loudness from the piano note it follows.

VocalTractLab, a model of the human vocal tract, sings it as you listen, here in your browser: the lips, tongue and glottis move, and the sound comes from the air passing through them.

Exaggeration

How far the performance departs from the plain score, in tempo, rubato, dynamics, accents, articulation, arpeggios and pedalling. At 0 the roll plays without expression, at 1 as Grünfeld played it, at 2 with every deviation doubled; espressivo computes each from the reconstruction.

The voice always goes a little further than the piano, except in timing, which the two share. A new setting is rendered afresh and takes over at the same place in the piece.
